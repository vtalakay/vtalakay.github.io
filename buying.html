First Car Buying Guide

Buying a first car involves more than looking at the purchase price. Use this guide to help evaluate the total cost and practicality of a vehicle before making a purchase.

1. Set Your Budget

Determine how much you can comfortably spend on the vehicle. Remember to consider insurance, fuel, maintenance, registration, and potential repairs.

2. Research Reliability

Research the vehicle's history, maintenance requirements, common repair issues, and ownership costs. A vehicle that is affordable to purchase may have higher costs over time.

3. Check Safety Features

Look for important safety equipment such as airbags, electronic stability control, backup cameras, and available driver-assistance technology.

4. Consider Fuel Economy

Fuel costs can become a significant part of a driver's monthly expenses. Compare fuel economy estimates and consider how much the vehicle will be driven.

5. Inspect a Used Vehicle

If you are purchasing a used vehicle, review its maintenance records and vehicle history. Consider having an independent mechanic inspect the vehicle before completing the purchase.

6. Take a Test Drive

A test drive can help you evaluate visibility, comfort, braking, steering, acceleration, and overall driving feel.

First Car Checklist

  • Set a realistic budget
  • Research insurance costs
  • Compare fuel economy
  • Research reliability
  • Check vehicle history
  • Inspect the vehicle
  • Take a test drive
  • Review all purchase costs
